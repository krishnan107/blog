## A New Era for Prediction Markets

It is June 13, 2026, and the world of prediction markets is undergoing a significant transformation. If you have been following the rise of Kalshi, you know that the platform has become a go-to spot for betting on everything from interest rates to weather patterns. But with great growth comes great scrutiny. Today, Kalshi announced a series of new rules aimed at one of the biggest threats to any financial marketplace: insider trading.

## The Problem With Inside Information

For a long time, the allure of prediction markets was their speed and efficiency. People with specialized knowledge could weigh in on events, helping to create a market price that reflected the collective wisdom of the crowd. However, this model faces a major hurdle when that specialized knowledge is non-public or sensitive. If you work in a specific industry and have access to data that the general public does not, using that information to place a bet isn't just smart trading. It is insider trading, and it undermines the integrity of the platform.

Kalshi has seen enough suspicious activity to realize that the honor system is no longer sufficient. To keep the playing field fair for everyone, the platform is rolling out a new requirement for a subset of its users. Depending on your trading volume and the specific markets you frequent, you may now be required to disclose your current job details and professional affiliations.

## Why Your Job Title Matters

This move might sound invasive to some, but it is a standard practice in traditional finance. If you trade stocks, your brokerage is often required to know if you are an employee of a public company or a person with access to material non-public information. By bringing these rules to the prediction space, Kalshi is signaling that it wants to be taken seriously as a financial institution.

The goal is simple. By knowing who its users are and where they work, Kalshi can flag potential conflicts of interest before they become a scandal. If a user is betting heavily on a specific economic report while working at the firm that publishes it, the platform can now act. This transparency protects the average user who is just trying to make a calculated guess about the future.

## What This Means for You

If you are a casual user, you likely have nothing to worry about. Most of the new disclosures are targeted at "power users" or those operating in high-stakes environments. If you do fall into that category, expect to see prompts asking for your employer details the next time you log in to place a trade. While filling out extra forms is rarely fun, it is a small price to pay for a market that is resistant to manipulation.

Ultimately, these changes are a sign that prediction markets are maturing. As these platforms become more integrated into our financial lives, we should expect more oversight, not less. By requiring users to reveal their professional background, Kalshi is taking a necessary step to ensure that the markets remain a place for genuine forecasting rather than a playground for those with secret advantages.
