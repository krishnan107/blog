## A Scorching Milestone

Happy July 1, 2026! We are officially kicking off the week of the big 250th birthday celebration for the United States. While everyone is busy planning backyard barbecues, firework viewings, and trips to the beach, there is a significant weather update that everyone needs to keep on their radar. Forecasters are warning that a massive, dangerous heatwave is set to sweep across North America just as the holiday weekend arrives.

It is certainly not the ideal forecast for a historic milestone, but that does not mean we cannot celebrate. With temperatures expected to reach record highs in many regions, preparation is going to be the difference between a fun weekend and a trip to the urgent care clinic. The heat is expected to be persistent, meaning it will not cool down much even after the sun sets.

## Stay Safe in the Sun

If you have big plans for the Fourth of July, it is time to adjust your strategy. The most important rule during a heatwave is to limit your time outdoors, especially during the peak hours of 10 a.m. to 4 p.m. If you must be outside, seek out shade whenever possible and wear lightweight, light-colored clothing that reflects the sun. Sunscreen is also non-negotiable. A bad sunburn will only make your body struggle more to regulate its internal temperature.

Hydration is your best friend this week. Do not wait until you feel thirsty to drink water. Keep a bottle with you at all times and try to avoid sugary or caffeinated drinks, which can actually lead to faster dehydration. If you are hosting a party, make sure there is a designated cooling station or a misting fan set up for your guests. If your home does not have central air conditioning, consider spending the hottest parts of the day in a local library, mall, or movie theater.

## Watching Out for Others

Heat exhaustion and heat stroke are serious concerns during events like this. Keep a close eye on children, the elderly, and your pets. They are much more vulnerable to high temperatures than the average adult. If you see someone acting confused, feeling dizzy, or showing signs of heavy sweating followed by a lack of sweat, get them to a cool area immediately and seek medical help. Never leave a person or a pet in a parked car, even for a few minutes. Temperatures inside a vehicle can become lethal in a matter of seconds.

We are all excited to celebrate 250 years of history this weekend. Let us make sure that we reach the end of the holiday safely by respecting the heat and looking out for our neighbors. Stay hydrated, stay cool, and have a fantastic Fourth of July.
