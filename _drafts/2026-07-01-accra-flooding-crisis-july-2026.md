## A Difficult Start to July

It is July 1, 2026, and the mood in Accra is heavy. As the capital city of Ghana grapples with the aftermath of severe flooding, the news arriving this morning is deeply heartbreaking. At least 13 people have lost their lives in the deluge, leaving families to mourn and communities to pick up the pieces. The images coming from the ground show streets turned into rivers and homes overwhelmed by rising water, serving as a stark reminder of the vulnerability of our urban infrastructure during the rainy season.

## The Danger Is Not Over

If you are currently in the Accra area, please listen carefully to the warnings coming from local authorities. The rain might have paused momentarily, but the forecast is grim. Meteorologists are tracking another storm system that is expected to hit the region very soon. The ground is already saturated and cannot absorb any more water, which means the risk of flash flooding is extremely high. 

Officials are urging everyone to take immediate precautions. If you live in a low lying area or a place prone to drainage issues, please consider moving to higher ground if it is safe to do so. If you are able to stay home, please remain indoors and keep away from floodwaters, which can hide dangerous debris or electrical hazards. Do not attempt to drive through flooded streets, as even shallow water can hide deep potholes or manholes and can easily sweep a vehicle away.

## Coming Together in Tough Times

When disaster strikes, the strength of a community is always tested. Seeing citizens look out for their neighbors and emergency services working around the clock is a testament to the resilience of the Ghanaian people. However, resilience does not mean we should accept these tragedies as inevitable. This recurring cycle of flooding highlights the urgent need for better urban planning, improved drainage systems, and a more robust waste management strategy to ensure that debris does not choke the city waterways.

For now, the priority remains the safety of every resident. Check on your neighbors, especially the elderly and those living alone. Keep your mobile phones charged and stay tuned to local radio or reliable social media channels for real time updates from the Ghana Meteorological Agency. Avoid unnecessary travel and stay clear of areas known to experience heavy pooling. 

We are keeping the families of the victims in our thoughts today. We hope that the upcoming storm passes with minimal impact and that the city can begin the long process of recovery soon. Please stay safe and follow all official guidance until the danger has fully passed.
