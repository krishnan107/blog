## Is It Just Me, or Is It Hotter?

It is June 24, 2026, and if you have stepped outside today, you have likely noticed that the air feels different. There is a heaviness to the atmosphere that makes even a short walk to the mailbox feel like an endurance sport. You are not imagining things. While we are all used to the occasional spike in temperature during the summer months, this current heatwave carries a specific intensity that feels noticeably worse than the ones we have navigated in recent years.

## The Perfect Storm of Discomfort

Simon King recently broke down the science behind why this heatwave feels so much more oppressive. It is not just about the numbers on the thermometer. While the raw heat is certainly high, the discomfort stems from a combination of environmental factors that are working against us simultaneously.

First, we are dealing with a significant spike in humidity levels. When the air is saturated with moisture, our bodies lose their primary cooling mechanism, which is sweat evaporation. Because the moisture cannot evaporate into the already thick air, our internal temperatures climb faster. We feel sticky, sluggish, and far more fatigued than we would in a dry heat of the same temperature.

Second, we have to talk about the lack of nighttime relief. In past heatwaves, we could often count on temperatures dropping significantly after the sun went down. This allowed our homes and our bodies to reset before the next morning. During this event, however, the nighttime lows are remaining stubbornly high. Our infrastructure is essentially soaking up heat all day and failing to release it at night, which keeps the ambient temperature inside our homes elevated for days on end.

## A New Pattern of Heat

Beyond the immediate humidity and the lack of cooling, there is a cumulative effect at play. Many of us have spent the last few weeks moving through a series of smaller temperature fluctuations. By the time this major spike arrived, our bodies were already slightly taxed. We have less resilience to deal with extreme conditions when they arrive on the heels of an already warm and taxing start to the summer season.

It is also worth noting that wind patterns have been unusually stagnant. Without a steady breeze to move the air around, we are essentially stuck in a pocket of trapped heat. This lack of airflow prevents the passive cooling of buildings and makes the outdoor environment feel stagnant and suffocating.

## Stay Safe Out There

Understanding why this heat feels so intense is helpful, but it does not make the weather any easier to manage. Since we are stuck with these conditions for a little while longer, please prioritize your health. Keep hydrated, find the coolest room in your home, and check on neighbors who might be struggling with the high temperatures. This heatwave is testing our patience, but we will make it through to the cooler days ahead.
