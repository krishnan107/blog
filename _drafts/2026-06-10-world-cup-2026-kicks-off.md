The wait is finally over. After four years, the FIFA World Cup is back — and this time, it's bigger than ever. The 2026 tournament kicks off tomorrow, June 11, with a dream opening match that history is already attached to: **Mexico vs. South Africa** at the legendary Estadio Azteca in Mexico City.

## A Repeat 16 Years in the Making

Cast your mind back to June 11, 2010. The World Cup opened in Johannesburg with South Africa facing Mexico — and the Bafana Bafana stunned the world, holding Mexico to a 1–1 draw in front of a roaring home crowd.

Exactly 16 years later, the two sides meet again in a World Cup opener. This time the roles are reversed — Mexico is the co-host on home soil, and South Africa travel to Azteca as underdogs. The symmetry is remarkable. The storyline writes itself.

Kickoff is set for **3:00 PM ET / 12:00 PM PT** on June 11. The opening ceremony begins 90 minutes earlier at 1:30 PM, featuring performances from Shakira, Burna Boy, Belinda, and Danny Ocean.

## The Biggest World Cup Ever

The 2026 edition is a landmark tournament in every sense. For the first time, **48 nations** compete instead of the previous 32 — expanding the group stage to include more teams, more matches, and more stories. The tournament spans three co-host nations: the **United States, Mexico, and Canada**, making it the first World Cup ever held across three countries.

That means matches from Toronto to Los Angeles, from Guadalajara to New York. Stadiums that have never hosted a World Cup — MetLife Stadium in New Jersey, AT&T Stadium in Dallas, SoFi Stadium in Los Angeles — will become part of football history.

## Group A: Mexico's Road Begins

Mexico's group stage will be a test of nerves and home advantage. Group A consists of:

- 🇲🇽 **Mexico** (co-host)
- 🇿🇦 **South Africa**
- 🇰🇷 **South Korea**
- 🇨🇿 **Czechia**

Playing at Azteca — the stadium that has witnessed Diego Maradona's Hand of God and Pelé's coronation — Mexico will carry the weight of a nation's hope on their shoulders. Home crowd, familiar altitude, enormous pressure.

South Korea bring pace and discipline. Czechia are quietly dangerous in midfield. But it's that opening clash with South Africa that sets the tone.

This isn't just a football tournament. The 2026 World Cup is a statement — that the sport's reach has grown beyond its traditional European and South American heartlands. With 16 more nations competing than ever before, expect upsets. Expect drama. Expect the kind of moments that make non-fans suddenly care deeply about a country they'd struggle to find on a map.
