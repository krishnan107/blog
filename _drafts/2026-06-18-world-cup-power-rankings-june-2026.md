## The Wait Is Finally Over

It is June 18, 2026, and the football world is absolutely buzzing. We have officially seen every single one of the 48 teams take the pitch for their opening match of the tournament. The expansion to 48 teams brought a lot of skepticism, but after the first round of games, the drama has been undeniable. With the dust finally settling on the opening slate, the experts at BBC Sport have put their necks on the line to rank every single squad from first to worst.

## A New King at the Top

Usually, when we talk about tournament favorites, the conversation starts and ends with Argentina. As the defending champions, they carry a certain aura that is hard to ignore. However, after their opening performance, the experts have decided to look elsewhere for the top spot. It seems that while Argentina is still a force to be reckoned with, another nation has captured the imagination of the pundits with a more clinical and dominant display in their debut match.

Seeing someone other than Lionel Messi and his squad sitting at number one is a bold take. It reminds us that tournament football is about form, momentum, and finding that extra gear at the right moment. The rankings reflect not just the final scorelines, but the quality of play, the tactical discipline shown, and the sheer confidence displayed on the biggest stage of them all. Whether you agree with the top pick or think the experts have lost their minds, the list is sure to spark some heated debates in group chats across the globe.

## The View From the Bottom

Of course, for every team sitting pretty at the top of the rankings, someone has to occupy the basement. The expansion of the World Cup has given smaller nations a chance to shine, but it has also highlighted the massive gap in experience and depth between the elite and the newcomers. Some teams struggled to find their rhythm, while others looked overwhelmed by the intensity of the opening round. It is a harsh reality, but that is the nature of the beautiful game. The bottom tier of these rankings serves as a wake-up call for those squads as they head into their second matches.

## What Happens Next?

These rankings are just a snapshot in time. We know that the World Cup is a marathon, not a sprint. A team ranked 30th today could easily find their stride and make a deep run into the knockout stages. The beauty of this format is that everything can change with a single goal or a tactical adjustment. As we look ahead to the second round of fixtures, the pressure will only increase.

Whether your favorite team is sitting at number one or struggling at the bottom of the list, the tournament is only just beginning. We have plenty of football left to play, and if these opening games are any indication, we are in for a wild ride.
